The full plan

Three days, two nights, two travelers, three states. Two self-guided college visits (University of Vermont and Williams College) and two summit goals (Mount Mansfield, conditional, and Mount Greylock). About 500–550 miles and 10–11.5 hours of driving.

Day 1 · Saturday, November 21

West Hartford → Burlington → Stowe

6:45 AMDepart West Hartford. I-84 W / I-91 N to White River Junction, I-89 N to Burlington. 215–225 mi, 3 h 45 m–4 h, one optional rest stop.
10:45 AMBurlington, about 75 minutes: Church Street Marketplace, Lake Champlain waterfront, downtown shops and cafés.
12:00 PMLunch at Hong's Chinese Dumplings, 77 Pearl Street (12:00–12:45). Saturday hours listed as 12:00–7:00 PM.
1:00 PMUniversity of Vermont, self-guided and outdoors (1:00–3:00): University Green, Waterman Building, Old Mill, Bailey/Howe Library, Davis Center, Redstone Campus, athletic facilities. No building access or admissions staff assumed.

Saturday driving: about 250–265 mi and 4.5–5 hours, including local travel.

Day 2 · Sunday, November 22

Mount Mansfield → Williamstown

6:15 AMDepart for the authorized Mount Mansfield access point at Stowe Mountain Resort (15–20 min).
6:35 AMCheck uphill permissions, trail and parking restrictions, wind, temperature, visibility, snow and ice. Stowe Mountain Resort: 802-253-3690. No closed or unauthorized ski trails. Do not assume Route 108 through Smugglers' Notch is open.
7:00 AMBegin the summit attempt. Mount Mansfield, 4,393 ft, Vermont's highest point (the Chin).
10:30 AMTarget summit and firm reassessment.
11:00 AMTurnaround deadline.
12:30–1:30Expected return to the trailhead. Dry clothes, lunch, water, vehicle check.
2:00 PMDrive to Williamstown, 120–140 mi, 2 h 30 m–3 h 15 m, on a winter-maintained route chosen by live navigation.
5:00 PMCheck in at Williamstown Motel, 295 Main Street. 5:30–6:30 dinner; 6:30–7:30 prepare for Greylock; 8:30 sleep.

For reference, the Long Trail South approach is about 4.7 miles round trip with 2,790 ft of gain, roughly 5 hours in normal conditions. Winter can add a lot of time. The actual route depends on authorized access and conditions. Sunrise is about 6:55 AM and sunset about 4:17 PM; finishing by 1:30 leaves close to three hours of daylight.

The summit is conditional. If access is prohibited, visibility is poor, winds are excessive, or conditions exceed demonstrated skills and equipment, the summit attempt is cancelled for a lower-risk Stowe-area hike, and the rest of the trip goes ahead.

Sunday driving: about 140–160 mi and 3–3.5 hours, including local travel.

Day 3 · Monday, November 23

Williams College → Mount Greylock → West Hartford

6:30 AMWake up, breakfast, check out, pack the car.
7:15 AMWilliams College, self-guided (7:15–8:30), starting at Weston Hall as the college recommends: Main Quad, Thompson Memorial Chapel, Sawyer Library, Paresky Center, Spring Street, athletic facilities. Campus map downloaded in advance; buildings may not be open yet.
8:30 AMDrive to the Hopper Road trailhead (10–15 min).
8:45 AMBegin Mount Greylock via the Hopper Trail. 3,491 ft, Massachusetts' highest point.
11:15 AMTarget summit: Veterans War Memorial, photos, short break.
11:30 AMTurnaround deadline if the summit has not been reached.
~2:00 PMBack at the trailhead.
2:15 PMDrive home, 110–125 mi, 2 h 15 m–2 h 45 m.
4:30–5:00Arrive in West Hartford.

From the Hopper Road trailhead (open year-round), the state's trail mileages put the summit 4.1 miles away: 2.4 miles to the Sperry Road campground, then 1.7 miles on to the summit. That makes about 8.2 miles round trip with roughly 2,400 ft of gain. The summit auto road closes after October 30, so the summit is reached on foot, and the memorial tower is closed for the season. Blaze orange on both hikes: it is hunting season.

Safety

Accommodations

  • Two predetermined overnight locations, indoors both nights.
  • No improvised sleeping locations.
  • Parents receive both addresses and phone numbers.

Driving

  • Vehicle checked before departure; winter-ready tires and windshield equipment.
  • Full tank before mountain travel; navigation downloaded offline.
  • No driving after 11 PM.
  • The passenger handles navigation and music; no phone use by the driver.

Winter hiking equipment

  • Winter hiking boots, insulated layers, waterproof shell, gloves and backup gloves.
  • Traction matched to actual conditions. Microspikes are not enough for steep, hard ice; crampons and an ice axe only where required and only with demonstrated training.
  • Headlamps, offline maps and compass, first aid and emergency insulation, food and water reserves.

Weather and mountain decisions

  • Mountain forecasts checked before both hikes; Stowe uphill permissions confirmed.
  • Fixed turnaround times. We do not separate.
  • The Mansfield summit is cancelled if weather, access or technical conditions are unsafe.
  • Off both mountains well before sunset.

Parent communication

  • Check-ins: leaving West Hartford, arriving in Burlington, finishing UVM, checking in at the Commodores Inn, starting and finishing Mansfield, checking in at the Williamstown Motel, finishing Williams, finishing Greylock, arriving home.
  • Live location shared the whole trip, with a backup plan if there is no cell service.

Emergencies

  • 911. Both travelers carry charged phones, power banks and offline maps.
  • First-aid kit, parent emergency contacts, roadside assistance information.
  • Both travelers know the itinerary and the planned alternatives.

Winter summits are conditional on access, weather and demonstrated technical ability. A safe alternative is always better than forcing a summit.

To confirm before booking

  • Check-in age policy at both properties, plus availability and November rates.
  • Stowe Mountain Resort's 2026–27 uphill access routes for November 22 (802-253-3690).
  • Connecticut teen-driver passenger and curfew rules, if the driver is under 18.
  • Route 108 status and the mountain forecast on the morning of each hike.
  • Traction gear matched to the conditions reported that week.
